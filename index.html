MY JOURNEY & EVOLUTION

Evolution of IT Knowledge & Skills

Q1 Prior IT Knowledge Before BSIT

Before entering the BSIT program at Datamex College of Saint Adeline, my understanding of Information Technology was predominantly consumer-level. I was familiar with basic desktop operations, routine OS installations, and casual software usage, but I viewed technology as an end-user rather than an engineer. I understood that data traveled across the internet and that routers provided wireless access, but I had virtually no comprehension of the intricate routing protocols, Layer 2 frame switching, packet encapsulation, or architectural paradigms that power mission-critical communications.

Q5 How My Technical Skills Changed

Throughout my academic journey, my skillset underwent a complete paradigm shift from basic digital literacy to disciplined engineering competence. Today, I do not merely operate devices—I analyze network telemetry, design segmented VLAN topologies, calculate complex VLSM subnet allocations, and isolate hardware bottlenecks with structured OSI-model diagnostic methodology. Earning my NC II in Computer Systems Servicing and leading administrative system deployments proved that my skills had graduated from textbook theory into deployable, enterprise-ready technical competencies.

Core Academic Learnings & Systems Thinking

Q2 The Most Important Things I Learned

The most profound lessons I acquired extend beyond syntax or specific software. First, I mastered root-cause diagnostic methodology—the discipline of isolating failures systematically from the physical cabling up through transport and application layers rather than relying on trial and error.

Second, I learned defensive systems design, understanding how unmonitored switch ports, misconfigured ACLs, and unsanitized queries introduce severe security vulnerabilities. Third, I discovered the vital role of technical documentation and process automation; building an infrastructure is only sustainable when properly mapped with network topology diagrams, comprehensive READMEs, and verifiable audit trails.

Capstone Projects & Technical Adversity

Q3 Which Project Taught Me the Most & Why

The project that contributed most substantially to my technical growth was the AI-Assisted Computer Repair Shop Management System. It forced me outside isolated frontend design into real-world systems engineering: binding responsive client UI components to cloud databases (Supabase/PostgreSQL) and integrating an automated Gemini AI diagnostic engine. Unlike small classroom exercises, this full-stack system required end-to-end data integrity, ticket state transitions, client-side validation, and resilient error recovery.

Q4 The Technical Difficulty That Challenged Me Most

My most daunting technical challenge occurred during real-time database state synchronization and handling asynchronous AI API latency. When network interruptions occurred, unhandled promises caused UI freezes and inconsistent ticket statuses. I overcame this obstacle by architecting structured try-catch-finally wrappers, implementing exponential backoff retry algorithms in api/diagnose.js, and designing optimistic UI updates. This trial demystified distributed systems and taught me that bulletproof error handling separates hobby projects from commercial software.

Continuous Growth & Industry Readiness

Q6 Skills I Still Need to Improve

Recognizing that technology constantly shifts, I acknowledge clear growth areas. I am actively training to deepen my expertise in Network Automation using Python (Netmiko/NAPALM), Infrastructure as Code (Terraform), and Enterprise Multi-VPC Cloud Routing (AWS Transit Gateway & Direct Connect). Scaling enterprise networks requires programmatic configuration over manual CLI execution.

Q7 The Kind of IT Professional I Want to Become

I aspire to become a respected Senior Network Infrastructure & Security Engineer who architects zero-downtime, zero-trust network backbones. I want to be recognized for unflinching technical integrity, structured incident containment, and the ability to articulate complex network architectures clearly to both executive stakeholders and technical peers.

Q8 Why I Am Ready to Begin My IT Career

I am prepared to launch my career in Information Technology because my knowledge is anchored by verifiable evidence: “I learned this. I built this. Here is the evidence. Here is my source code. Here is the live system.” Armed with an NC II credential, real-world project leadership, functional capstone systems, and a passion for network engineering, I am equipped to contribute immediate, measurable value to an enterprise network operations center (NOC) or IT infrastructure team from day one.
